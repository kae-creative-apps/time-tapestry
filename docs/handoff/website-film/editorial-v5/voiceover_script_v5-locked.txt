Start with your own story, or invite someone you love. Choose who you'd like to share it with. You'll answer one question at a time about the people who cared for you and how you lived your faith. There's also room for the ways you gave to others, and what you hope to pass on. Your answers become four stories. Read the drafts, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and approve what you're ready to share. Your family scans the postcard to open your stories and leave a reply. Time Tapestry. Stories woven together.

SARAH PERFORMANCE DIRECTION

Keep Sarah. Speak warmly and naturally to one person sitting nearby, as if explaining why you made something for your own family. Use an ordinary speaking voice, close and clear, with gentle conviction rather than a presenter cadence. Aim for about 150 words per minute during speech. Connect the sentences and breathe where you need to, without long pauses or equal emphasis on every clause. Let the opening sound like a real motivation. Treat faith as an ordinary part of a lived story. Give review and approval normal, clear emphasis. Finish the name and tagline simply. Do not whisper, perform sadness, or read the clauses as separate slogans.
