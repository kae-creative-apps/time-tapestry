# Time Tapestry
## Designer handoff · October 6, 2026 · v1

Use the supplied logo artwork as the source of truth. Recommendations below are proposals, not a new approved identity.

## The idea to preserve

**Name:** Time Tapestry
**Tagline:** Stories woven together.

Time Tapestry helps people pass on the stories, faith and everyday acts of generosity that reveal who they are. The gift is the person: their voice, the choices they made and what they hope the next generation carries forward.

The two lowercase t’s suggest a stitch and a connection between generations. They should feel like joining hands and passing something onward. Preserve the supplied mark’s geometry and connection. Do not replace it with a generic spool, sewing needle or infinity symbol.

Tayloe’s practical design test is simple: could an older family member with little technology experience understand the next step without help?

## Confirmed direction

- **Warm, personal and clear.** Generous space, readable type and one obvious next action. Use names where they help a gift feel personal.
- **Clearly Christian and welcoming.** Faith belongs naturally in the brand. Faith questions and difficult memories remain optional. Avoid making someone prove their faith or generosity.
- **Living legacy.** Invite stories while relationships can keep growing. Avoid grief pressure, countdowns or language that assumes someone has died.
- **The storyteller’s own voice.** Finished family films use recorded audio or video from the person. Never replace their voice with synthesized narration or have a narrator identify itself as AI. In public copy, call the experience a conversation or guided interview, not an “AI interviewer.” Required transparency and consent still belong in the appropriate product screens.
- **Faithful editing.** Improve clarity while retaining meaning, character and context. Use genuine source quotations. Never invent dialogue, memories, relationships or spiritual conclusions.
- **Quiet motion.** The logo animation should communicate the two t’s connecting and passing a story onward. Movement should settle gracefully into the existing logo.

## What the designs need to explain

The original gift begins with four recorded chapters: **Kindness received; A life of faith; What you sowed; What I hope you carry.** The intended outputs are four films, a branded PDF book and four postcards to one chosen postal recipient. Postcard encouragement can be personalized; story answers are recorded rather than typed or edited as a transcript.

The postcard schedule is **day 0, 14, 28 and 42**, spanning six weeks. These are scheduled sending intervals, not promised arrival dates. QR codes lead to private stories accessed through email verification.

After the original gift is completed and shared, more recorded stories can join the family collection and book. The implemented prompt library contains 100 original questions across **Character, Health, Relationships, Finances, Happiness, Meaning and Faith**, inspired by Gloo’s framework. Family members can request up to three at a time; storytellers can choose their own. Present an organized collection with search and clear chapter titles.

Implementation is not proof of completed live delivery. Do not publish promises of guaranteed mail dates, permanent hosting, flawless automated editing or a single combined video download.

## Design work still to resolve

The supplied postcard proofs accurately show the current implementation: **one shared front and four different backs**. They use fictional names and inactive QR codes.

Tayloe has requested four distinct but related fronts, brighter and less brown, with a complete, more transparent woven pattern. Pink and green are not the requested direction. Propose a restrained family of variations using the existing identity, ample contrast and a quieter pattern behind text. Keep the logo consistent across the series. These new fronts require review before replacing the current artwork.
