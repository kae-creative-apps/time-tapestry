## The creative decision

Open with a human question or moment: who taught someone to give, welcome a stranger, persevere or pray. If a cleared real recording is available, let the audience hear a short original excerpt. Otherwise use a narrator-led question and illustrative footage. Do not fabricate an interview quotation as customer proof.

By about ten seconds, show what the product does. A search interface or research statistic can support the explanation later if relevant. Neither should carry the opening simply to establish importance. This is a creative recommendation informed by research, not a proven conversion result for Time Tapestry.

The emotional progression should be: I recognize someone I love; there is something I want to ask them; this makes it easy to hear the answer; I can respond; we have another reason to talk.

## Research and its limits

### A story can hold attention and support persuasion

A meta-analysis of narrative communication found advantages for narratives on several immediate and delayed persuasion measures. That supports testing a concrete memory before abstract feature explanation. It does not establish that a sentimental opening will win this competition or that stories always outperform factual explanation. Pair the story with evidence. [Oschatz and Marker, Journal of Communication, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/70/4/473/5880130)

### Follow-up questions communicate that someone listened

Three studies reported a connection between question-asking, perceived responsiveness and liking, with follow-up questions especially important. Applied here, a family member asking about something they just heard could make the storyteller feel that their experience mattered. This is a product hypothesis extrapolated from conversational studies, not a tested result for asynchronous family recordings. [Huang and colleagues, 2017](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Huang%20et%20al%202017_6945bc5e-3b3e-4c0a-addd-254c9e603c60.pdf)

### Original voice is part of the value

Experiments comparing voice-based and text-based interactions found stronger connection through voice in those settings. The application to recorded family stories is an inference, but it gives a sensible design priority: make hearing the real person a central demonstration. Do not substitute a synthetic reading to make an example easier to produce. [University of Texas account of Kumar and Epley's research](https://news.mccombs.utexas.edu/research/when-we-fear-an-awkward-phone-call-we-often-turn-to-text/)

### Family stories can help people understand their place in a larger history

Emory's family-narrative research connects coherent, emotionally open family storytelling with aspects of identity and wellbeing. The reported studies have population and design limits; they do not show that this product causes resilience or improves mental health. Show ordinary acts of faith, generosity, humor and difficulty, without making clinical claims or forcing every memory into a happy ending. [Emory Family Narratives Lab research overview](https://news.emory.edu/stories/2020/04/how-family-stories-help-children-weather-hard-times)

### Gloo needs useful, evidenced technology

Gloo describes the 2026 event as emphasizing applied AI for real ministry and community needs, with values alignment and human flourishing. The strongest presentation therefore pairs the family moment with a real completed workflow and a precise explanation of what the AI contributes. Exact judging weights, presentation rules and private challenge requirements were not verified. The project currently specifies a 90-second film limit; confirm that against the actual submission instructions. [Official 2026 announcement](https://gloo.com/press/releases/gloo-announces-2026-ai-hackathon)

## The new feature worth prioritizing

Build the complete family-requested story experience before broadening the catalog.

1. After watching, the recipient can reply or choose something they would like to hear next.
2. Show three relevant suggested questions, with a browse-more option for the larger library. Examples for testing: Who helped you through a hard season? What did your parents teach you about giving? Is there a prayer you want to share with us?
3. The storyteller receives the request through the verified account and email. They can record an answer, defer it or decline it.
4. The new recording is saved durably and added as a dated story. Keep the original four chapters and original recordings intact.
5. The storyteller selects the audience. Added email stories must not trigger extra physical postcards or new charges implicitly.
6. Notify selected family members and allow replies.

The user-facing promise is that a story can lead to another conversation. Avoid treating the family as an engagement metric. Useful pilot measures are completed recordings, film/PDF readiness, successful invited access, replies, and voluntary second-story recordings. Measure family experience directly before making wellbeing claims.

## Voice consistency

The homepage selected take 2, organizations take 1, and Gloo take 4. All three are separate ElevenLabs Music v2.5 generations combining spoken words and score. No shared reusable voice ID is recorded. Earlier Sarah TTS references are a different production method and must not silently replace the approved sound.

Keeping an existing master preserves its exact performance. New words require new audio. Reference conditioning may help new audio stay close to the approved recording, but it is not an exact voice-identity guarantee. The current ElevenLabs documentation describes both unchanged reference chunks and generated chunks conditioned on stored audio. Account availability and actual matching quality need a short test. [ElevenLabs music inpainting](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/music/inpainting)

Preserve the approved masters, test a brief new passage first, compare by listening, and only then render revised full tracks. Keep the desired energy in the interpretation, rather than changing narrator character. Separate narration and music where feasible for future revisions. Do not rush the narrator to fit new features into an old runtime.

The Gloo export's old snapshot incorrectly names its v1 master. Registry and audio comparison establish that the delivered export uses the selected v2 master. Correct that provenance note during the film revision, without replacing the delivered take.
