# True Legacy

# **The evidence for carrying generosity forward**

Presentation statistics, donor preferences, church and nonprofit incentives, and what to validate next. Version 2 • September 30, 2026

## **Start the presentation with these six facts**

| Story to tell | Presentation statistic | Source and boundary |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Older adults care about what they pass on.** | 43% of US adults 50+ named life lessons and values among their most important legacies. | Edward Jones / Age Wave / Harris Poll, 2021 \[18\]. Broad legacy preference, not a generosity-specific question. |
| **Church leaders see a need.** | 51% of Protestant senior pastors were very concerned about younger Christians not financially supporting the church; another 43% were somewhat concerned (94% combined). | Barna, 516 pastors, 2022 fieldwork \[19\]. Concern does not establish demand for an app. |
| **Young adults investigate where money goes.** | 81% of Gen Z (and 80% of Elders) reported researching organizations before offering financial support. | Barna, 2021 adult survey \[20\]. Historical self-report, not a current conversion rate. |
| **Young people already contribute.** | 54% of Gen Z reported volunteering for an organization in the prior three months. | Barna, 2021 adult survey \[3\]. Generosity includes time and service. |
| **Family conversations are a credible starting point.** | IU reports children whose parents discuss giving are 20% more likely to give. | Women Give 2013 \[1\]. Association; not 20 percentage points or a True Legacy outcome. |
| **Organizations need to develop participation.** | The number of donors fell an estimated 3.6% in 2025, continuing a decline since 2021, even as dollars raised rose 5.0%. | Fundraising Effectiveness Project \[22\]. Its tracked nonprofit data, not a census of all US donors. |

For a short demo, use the first, second, and fifth facts. Keep the others for the supporting slide or questions. Every figure needs its year and population on the slide. Linked source details follow.

## **The proposition to test**

True Legacy helps churches guide families through the stories behind their generosity, then choose how to carry it forward together. The research supports this direction. It does not yet prove product demand, increased giving, or lasting behavior change.

# **Is the idea well backed?**

Yes, as a reason to build and test a focused pilot. The evidence is strongest for a real organizational concern, existing interest in meaningful legacy, and the relevance of family conversations. The evidence is weaker for the specific leap from an AI-guided story to sustained generosity or church revenue.

| Claim | Assessment | What we can responsibly say |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Older people want to leave something meaningful.** | Supported, with scope limits. | The legacy survey measures memories and values. It does not isolate generosity or churchgoing grandparents. |
| **Churches care about younger generations’ generosity.** | Direct evidence of concern. | The pastor survey supports the problem. It does not show willingness to buy True Legacy. |
| **Family behavior and conversation relate to giving.** | Supported by longitudinal observational research. | These relationships justify a family-centered pilot. They do not establish causation for this product. |
| **Young adults have distinct giving preferences.** | Supported directionally. | Research and relational learning matter. Generation, age, income and life stage are intertwined. |
| **AI interviewing is better than existing alternatives.** | Unproven. | Compare it with a simple question sheet or a family-led recording. |
| **True Legacy increases giving or retention.** | Unproven. | No verified True Legacy usage, revenue, retention or donation results were available. |

## **Suggested opening narrative**

“Many older adults want to pass on the values that shaped their lives. Church leaders are concerned about how the next generation will practice generosity. Families offer a natural place to begin: research links conversations about giving with children’s charitable behavior. True Legacy gives a family a simple way to hear the story behind someone’s generosity, respond in their own words, and choose a next step together.”

This is a synthesis of \[18\], \[19\] and \[1\], followed by a product proposal. It is not a claim that those researchers studied or endorsed True Legacy.

## **The strongest positioning change**

Make carrying generosity forward the central promise. The saved story is useful because it creates an opening for a conversation. In the demo, show the younger person responding and choosing an action, so the experience has a clear purpose beyond storage.

Use “a legacy of generosity” as the mission. Avoid presenting it as an already measured market demand or promising that children will support the same institution as their parents.

# **Older generations and family influence**

## **A desire to pass on values**

Beyond the 43% values finding on page 1, 64% of adults 50+ prioritized memories from shared experiences and 32% an inheritance. These are overlapping preferences, not parts of a total. The nationally representative weighted online survey covered 2,020 US adults, August 12–16, 2021; the older subgroup size was not disclosed in the release. It was commissioned by Edward Jones and Age Wave and conducted by Harris Poll. Pandemic timing may affect responses.

This supports an invitation about meaning and family. Younger adults agree: in a 2020 Edward Jones / Age Wave survey, 83% of non-retired adults said memories, values and life lessons are the most important things to receive (75% of retirees said the same about passing them on). The generosity-specific desire still needs direct validation with intended users. Figures and methods are confirmed in the Edward Jones Four Pillars fall 2021 report, Figure 9 (edwardjones.com).

[\[18\] Edward Jones / Age Wave / Harris Poll • Legacy survey, 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/pandemic-prompted-first-time-legacy-planning-conversations-for-44-5-million-americans-edward-jones-finds-301398039.html)

## **What family-giving research adds**

Women Give 2013 followed 903 children using 2002–03 and 2007–08 data. IU’s summary reports the 20% greater likelihood cited on page 1 and says talking was more influential than parental modeling alone. The summary was reviewed; the model was not independently reanalyzed. This is older research on parents and children, not a study of AI. A 2016 Vanguard Charitable / IU study found grandparents’ and adult grandchildren’s giving priorities are less closely matched than parents’ and children’s, which is the gap a grandparent-led conversation would try to close.

[\[1\] Indiana University • Women Give 2013 research summary](https://philanthropy.indianapolis.iu.edu/news-events/news/_news/2013/women-give-2013.html)

Women Give 2018 examined 3,700 adult children, ages 19–65, across seven biennial waves from 2001–2013. Its regression-adjusted predicted giving rates were 80.5% when parents gave and 71.8% when they did not, an 8.7 percentage-point difference. Crucially, this definition excludes donations to religious congregations. It supports family influence on charitable behavior, not a forecast of church giving. For religious giving specifically, a 2016 Vanguard Charitable / IU study found parent and adult-child religious giving are positively linked.

[\[2\] Women’s Philanthropy Institute • Women Give 2018, pp. 7–9](https://www.ncfp.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Transmitting-Generosity-Women-Give-2018.pdf)

## **Wealth transfer is context, not the market size**

Cerulli’s December 2024 projection anticipates about \$124 trillion moving to heirs and charities through 2048, including about \$18 trillion to charity. Roughly 81% of projected transfers originate with Boomers and older generations; more than half comes from the roughly 2% of households classified as high or ultra-high net worth. These are forecasts, with rounded components, not committed gifts or software revenue.

[\[28\] Cerulli Associates • Wealth transfer projection, December 2024](https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/cerulli-anticipates-124-trillion-in-wealth-will-transfer-through-2048)

## **How this changes the product**

Ask for a specific memory: who helped you, what did you learn, and what do you hope your family carries forward? Include an older mentor when a relative is unavailable. Preserve the original voice, let the narrator correct the text, and let the younger recipient choose their own expression of generosity. An inherited value does not require an identical cause or donation amount.

# **What next-generation donors prefer**

| Evidence | Product decision to test |
| :---- | :---- |
| **81% of Gen Z research organizations before giving; 58% cite close friends and family as a source of financial learning \[20\].** | Pair a personal story with accessible information about any optional cause. Include a real family exchange. |
| **Annual charitable giving, including churches: Gen Z 51%, Millennials 61%, Gen X 58%, Boomers 64%, Elders 72% \[3\].** | Do not equate a lower donation rate with disinterest. Allow an action that costs no money. |
| **Routine nonprofit support: Gen Z 30%, Millennials 33% \[3\].** | Test a gentle follow-up after the family conversation. Do not assume a recorded story creates a recurring habit. |
| **The trust study reports 33% of Gen Z completely trust Christian churches with financial support, while nearly one in five trust them not at all \[27\].** | Explain the church’s role and family control clearly. A church invitation will help some people and deter others. |

Barna’s research and financial-learning findings \[20\] use a weighted online panel of 2,016 US adults, November 12–19, 2021, published November 2023\. Its giving and volunteering figures \[3\] use the same period and sample, published August 2023\. These are not independent confirmations. The stated overall margin of error is ±2 points; subgroup precision is not supplied.

[\[20\] Barna • Is Now a Good Time to Give? Today’s Young Adults Think So, 2023](https://www.barna.com/research/young-adults-generous/)  |  [\[3\] Barna • How Millennials and Gen Z Are Stepping into Generosity](https://www.barna.com/research/young-adults-giving/)

The trust article \[27\], published September 2023, lists several studies, including 2021 and 2023 adult surveys and a youth survey, without assigning every chart to a fieldwork wave. Use these trust figures as qualified context rather than a lead statistic. They do not support the blanket claim that young adults distrust churches.

[\[27\] Barna • Where Do Young Donors Place Trust?, 2023](https://www.barna.com/research/donors-care-about-credibility/)

## **What the experience should respect**

Younger participants should be able to respond, ask a question, choose a different cause, serve, give, or decide to continue the conversation later. Make any charitable destination transparent and optional. Keep stories private unless the people involved choose otherwise. These are product recommendations informed by the evidence, not preferences directly tested for True Legacy.

## **What we should not infer**

A younger cohort’s lower donation rate today does not predict its lifetime giving. Cross-sectional comparisons mix age, resources, family circumstances and generation. The volunteer figure does not establish that volunteering happens before donating. Neither research behavior nor mobile familiarity establishes a preference for AI interviews, automated donation prompts or public storytelling.

# **Why churches and organizations may use it**

## **There is a measured church concern**

The pastor findings on page 1 come from 516 US Protestant senior pastors surveyed March 25–April 5, 2022, with weighting for denomination, region and church size. Published October 2023, the research was produced with Gloo and Kingdom Advisors. It is highly relevant to this hackathon, but sponsorship is not product endorsement or independent validation. It does not represent every denomination or all faith communities.

[\[19\] Barna • Breaking Down Pastors’ Concerns About Church Giving, 2023](https://www.barna.com/research/future-generosity/)

There is also an opening: in a March 2026 Barna article, 45% of 507 Protestant senior pastors reported increased Gen Z engagement over the prior year; 42% reported increased Millennial engagement. Engagement was locally interpreted and not formally defined. The public article does not give fieldwork dates. This is pastor perception, not a measured national increase in giving.

[\[26\] Barna • Young Adult Church Engagement, March 2026](https://www.barna.com/trends/young-adults-church-engagement-pastors/)

## **A concrete adoption case to validate**

| Potential buyer | Incentive and practical offer | Evidence of value to collect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Church discipleship or family ministry leader** | Run a short family generosity cohort with guided prompts, private sharing and a conversation guide. | Families completing a conversation; younger recipients responding; leader requests to repeat. |
| **Church generosity or stewardship leader** | Create a relationship-based entry point for younger adults and include service alongside financial giving. | Voluntary next actions and follow-through; recurring participation over several months. |
| **Nonprofit development leader** | Help existing supporters explain why a cause matters and invite family members to explore it voluntarily. | Consented new relationships, event or service participation, and later retention. |
| **Legacy or planned-giving team** | Offer a values-story experience alongside established donor stewardship. | Supporter satisfaction and opt-in follow-up requests. Keep estate advice with qualified professionals. |
| **Small ministry operations team** | Use a repeatable workflow instead of arranging every interview and reminder manually. | Actual staff time per participating family, support burden and cost per completed conversation. |

These are adoption hypotheses, not demonstrated returns. The first buyer should be one church leader who can recruit a small cohort and owns a relevant budget. The leader dashboard should show aggregate progress; private family content should not become fundraising intelligence.

| **US charitable dollars, 2025 \[21\]** | \$617.20 billion; \+5.7% nominal and \+3.0% after inflation. | Aggregate giving grew. This does not measure how many people gave. |
| **Giving to religion, 2025 \[21\]** | \$151.58 billion; \+2.4% nominal and −0.2% after inflation. | Effectively flat purchasing power. About 24.6% of total giving, calculated as 151.58 ÷ 617.20. |

# **How True Legacy can help**

The proposed contribution is a repeatable family conversation that connects inherited values with a freely chosen action. That is a smaller and more credible claim than reversing a national decline in donor participation.

| Step | What to build in the first week | What it could improve |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Invite** | A church leader creates a cohort; an adult family member invites a relative or mentor. | A clear reason to begin and a person who can help. |
| **Remember** | Three voice prompts about receiving help, choosing generosity and lessons learned. | A concrete story rather than an abstract instruction to give. |
| **Review** | Original audio, editable transcript or summary, narrator approval. | Fidelity to the person’s meaning and control over sharing. |
| **Respond** | A private reply or question from the recipient. | An exchange between generations rather than passive viewing. |
| **Act** | Choose a conversation, service activity, act of care or optional gift. | A next step suitable for different resources and interests. |
| **Follow through** | A simple check-in and aggregate cohort progress. | A way to learn whether a chosen action actually happened. |

## **What could make it stand out**

Voice capture, AI summaries and family sharing are established features. Storyworth’s Magic Interviews already conducts guided phone interviews with follow-up questions and turns them into a polished narrative; Remento’s Speech-to-Story turns recordings into readable stories with family participation. Generous Giving already serves churches through facilitated generosity experiences. True Legacy needs a specific workflow and measured usefulness, not a claim that faith or storytelling alone makes it unique.

[\[9\] Storyworth • Voice recording and guided calls](https://help.storyworth.com/can-i-voice-record-my-stories)  |  [\[10\] Remento • How it works](https://www.remento.co/how-it-works)  |  [\[15\] Generous Giving • Church pathway](https://generousgiving.org/church/)

The strongest distinction to test is the complete church-supported family experience: an approved story about generosity, a recipient’s response, and a shared action. It should be easier for a leader to run and more meaningful for a family than a worksheet. Neither advantage has yet been demonstrated.

## **Keep the one-week build small**

Kaelyn: complete and test this single flow, including access control, mobile recording and error recovery. Tayloe: recruit one leader and a few adult family pairs, write the invitation and prompts, observe usage, and build the demo around a real consented example or clearly labeled fictional data.

Defer payment processing, CRM connections, donor scoring, voice cloning, video production and a public story feed. The demo should show the family outcome and clearly distinguish working features from planned ones.

# **What evidence to collect next**

Published research establishes context. A short pilot must establish usability, family value and a buyer’s commitment. All targets below are proposed decisions for this team, not research benchmarks or achieved results.

| Question | Measure and denominator | Proposed decision rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Can people complete it?** | Narrators approving a story ÷ narrators who start. Report raw counts too. | Observe at least five intended older users. Fix recurring blockers before more invitations. |
| **Does it reach another generation?** | Stories with a recipient reply within seven days ÷ stories shared at least seven days earlier. | For ten family pairs, look for at least six replies. Treat this as a practical target, not a population estimate. |
| **Does it create a conversation?** | Families reporting a two-way discussion within 14 days ÷ all enrolled families with 14 days of follow-up. | For ten pairs, aim for five discussions. Report nonresponse and reasons for stopping. |
| **Does an action follow?** | Families reporting a completed chosen action within 30 days ÷ all enrolled families with 30 days of follow-up. | Report intention separately from completion. Do not claim verified giving from a button click. |
| **Is it useful to the leader?** | Setup time, weekly administration time and support requests. | Compare actual effort with the leader’s current approach. |
| **Will a buyer adopt?** | Budget owners agreeing to a specific next cohort, date and price. | Seek one concrete paid-pilot commitment after demonstrating value. A compliment or survey “yes” is not a sale. |

## **Validate the exact legacy assumption**

Ask older participants what they hope their family understands about their generosity, whether they have already shared it, and what would make this comfortable or uncomfortable. Ask younger recipients what felt meaningful, what felt like pressure, and what they would choose to do next. Observe the experience before explaining why the product ought to matter.

## **Test the AI contribution**

Use a small comparison with the same prompts delivered as a worksheet or family-led recording. Compare completion, editing burden, family preference and staff time. A small convenience pilot can reveal problems but cannot establish causal donation impact. Any later giving study needs longer follow-up, consent, a suitable comparison and a clearly defined outcome.

## **Claims to leave out of the pitch**

Do not promise increased donations, faith retention, reduced loneliness, guaranteed legacy continuity or automatic donor conversion. Do not label the wealth-transfer projection as market size. Say what the prototype does today and what the pilot is designed to learn.

## **Evidence quality and independence**

The macro estimates, household studies and donor database describe different populations and units. They should inform one narrative without being merged into one trend line. Several Barna articles reuse the same survey. Sponsor-backed surveys are useful evidence when their scope is visible, but they do not prove demand for this product.

The material update from version 1 is a clearer donor and organizational case: older-adult legacy preferences, younger-donor research behavior, direct pastor concern, recent donor participation trends and a qualified history of giving. The original document is preserved. This version is the recommended evidence brief for the presentation.
