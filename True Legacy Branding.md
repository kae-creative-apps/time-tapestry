**Brand idea**

**Generosity is a story before it is a gift.** Everything hangs on that. The competitors sell memory preservation (Storyworth) and storytelling technology (Remento). Churches sell stewardship. You sit between them: a family's story of generosity, handed to the next generation in a form they'll keep.

**Voice**

Warm, plain, unhurried. Sounds like a good letter, not an app. Three rules:

* Say "grandmother," "story," "hand down," "keep." Avoid "content," "legacy planning," "platform," "engagement."  
* Never make giving an obligation. The word "should" is banned. Generosity is described, never prescribed.  
* Write for two readers at once: an 80-year-old and a 22-year-old. If either would feel talked down to, rewrite.

Sample lines: "Five letters. One life of generosity." / "Ask her now. Keep it forever." / "For grandparents with a story, and grandchildren who will need it."

**Look**

Physical and honest. This is your analog-real direction applied to a product. Both competitors already own the "warm digital" space: Storyworth with hand-drawn illustrations and an earthy palette, Remento with a woven-tapestry identity. Don't compete there. Go more tactile than either.commarts+1

* **Paper first.** Uncoated cream stock, one ink color, letterpress-feel typography. The brand is the object in the mailbox, not the screen.  
* **Typography:** a warm serif for stories and headlines (Fraunces or Source Serif 4), a quiet sans for interface and labels (Inter or Source Sans 3). Set the grandparent's quotes in the serif, large.  
* **Color:** one accent only. A deep oxblood or forest green on cream, with warm gray text. No bright saturated colors; they read as tech.  
* **Photography:** real hands, real letters, real kitchen tables, film grain. Never stock "happy multigenerational family" shots.  
* **A mark that is also a seal.** Design the logo so it works as an embossed wax-seal-style stamp on the envelope. It should feel like a signature, not a startup logo.

**The unboxing is the brand**

The mailer does the marketing. Each envelope should feel like it came from a person, not a company: handwriting-style address, the grandparent's first name on the flap, the sponsor's credit small and on the back ("Sent with love by Grace Church"). Number the five chapters visibly so people want to complete the set. The reply card is the most important brand touchpoint. Make it beautiful enough that someone photographs it.
